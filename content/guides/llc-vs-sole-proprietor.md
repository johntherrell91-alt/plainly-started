---
title: "LLC vs sole proprietorship for freelancers"
date: 2026-10-14
---
## Sole proprietorship
- Exists automatically when you start working for yourself.
- No state filing fee to exist (local licenses may still apply).
- **No separation** between you and the business legally.

## Single-member LLC
- Requires a state filing and fee (e.g. [$300 in Texas](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms/205_boc.pdf)), and in some states ongoing taxes (e.g. [California's $800 annual tax](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html)).
- Can help separate business liabilities from personal assets when you keep finances separate and follow formalities.
- By default the IRS treats a single-member LLC as a "disregarded entity", so income is still reported on your personal return ([CA FTB overview](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html)).

## Plain-English rule of thumb
Low-risk, early-stage freelancing: a sole proprietorship is fine to start. Signing client contracts with real liability, or earning steadily: an LLC is worth pricing out. Talk to a local accountant or attorney for your situation.
